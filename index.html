The problem

Five questions the report has to answer

A walk-in orthopedic practice competes on access. If a patient waits too long they leave — and the visit, the imaging and the downstream procedure leave with them. But access is only half the story: two clinics can see identical volume and convert it into very different revenue, depending on payer mix and how well claims actually collect.

  1. Are access, demand and revenue moving in the right direction across locations?
  2. When — and under which provider conditions — do waits break target?
  3. Which payers convert visits into revenue most effectively?
  4. Where is unresolved motor-vehicle-accident exposure aging, and which site needs action first?
  5. Where is imaging captured in-house, and which sites refer revenue out?

What the data said

Findings

Figures below describe the synthetic dataset. Interpretations are hypotheses to investigate, not proven causes or implemented outcomes.

Access is broadly late, not occasionally catastrophic

Median door-to-provider is 41 minutes against a 30-minute target; P90 is 59 minutes. 96.8% of eligible visits exceed 30 minutes, but only 9.0% exceed 60. That is a whole distribution shifted right, not a long tail — which points at rooming and scheduling capacity rather than a handful of bad days.

Provider P90 ranges from 48 to 76 minutes. Investigate shared intake constraints and adjust provider comparisons for site, case mix, shift, and workload before assigning cause.

Motor Vehicle Accident is the revenue story — in both directions

MVA is 17.9% of visits but 23.4% of net revenue — a +5.5 pp positive share gap, and the highest net revenue per visit of any payer at $303 against a $232 book average.

It is also where the cash is stuck. MVA collects at 29.5% where every commercial and government payer collects near 90%, and it holds 70.8% of all unpaid A/R.

The receivable is old — and uniformly so

Of $8.45M outstanding across 27,865 open claims, 88% sits in the 121+ day bucket and 91.5% is 90+ days. Median days outstanding is 595.

Split by site, exposure differs — Oklahoma City $3.5M, Edmond $3.0M, Ardmore $1.9M — but the 90+ share is essentially identical everywhere (91.4% / 91.5% / 91.6%). The aging profile is uniform, so this is a process problem, not a site problem. Prioritise by dollars, not by which clinic looks worst.

Ardmore refers 100% of its MRI volume out

In-house MRI capture is 65.7% overall — but the average hides the finding. Edmond captures 84.7%, Oklahoma City 84.9%. Ardmore captures none: all 5,995 of its MRI orders go to an outside provider, because the site has no scanner.

That is a concrete, quantified capital question — the kind a volume-only dashboard never surfaces.

Recommendations

What to do about it

Findings are only worth the decisions they change. Each one names the action, the number that justifies it, and how you would know it worked — and they ship inside the report, not just on this page.

1 Fix intake throughput, not provider behaviour

Do: Re-sequence rooming and intake rather than coaching individual clinicians. Staff Saturday like a weekday.

Because: P90 runs 48–53 minutes Monday to Friday and 80 minutes on Saturday — a 60% jump on one day. The gap between the slowest and fastest provider is only 76 vs 48 minutes, yet 96.8% of visits breach target. When nearly every visit is late but no provider is an outlier, the constraint is the process every patient passes through.

Measure — median door-to-provider back under 30, and Saturday P90 within 10 minutes of the weekday figure.

2 Keep MVA — but collect it on a clock

Do: Stand up a dedicated MVA follow-up track with a hard escalation point before day 90.

Because: MVA has the highest modeled net revenue per visit — $303 per visit against a $232 average, and +5.5 pp more revenue share than visit share. It is also the worst on cash: 29.5% collection where others clear ~90%, holding 70.8% of unpaid A/R. Profitability is not established: evaluate costs, payment timing, and realized cash before making a payer strategy decision.

Measure — share of MVA balance aged 90+ days, currently 91.5%.

3 Treat the receivable as one process, not three sites

Do: Run one central follow-up workflow, sequenced by dollars — Oklahoma City $3.5M, Edmond $3.0M, Ardmore $1.9M.

Because: The 90+ share is 91.4% / 91.5% / 91.6% across the three sites — identical to within two-tenths of a point. Nothing about a location explains the aging, so site-specific interventions would be effort spent on noise. 88% of the $8.45M already sits past 120 days, where recovery falls off hardest, so speed matters more than volume of effort.

Measure — median days outstanding, currently 595.

4 Put a scanner in Ardmore — or find out why not

Do: Model in-house MRI capacity at Ardmore against lease, staffing and read costs.

Because: Ardmore refers out 5,995 of 5,995 MRI orders because it has no scanner. Edmond captures 84.7%, Oklahoma City 84.9%. Matching its sister sites would retain roughly 5,100 studies over the same reporting period that are referred out. Annualize volume and evaluate costs and capacity before using this assumption in a capital model.

Measure — Ardmore in-house capture, currently 0%, against the ~85% its sister sites already achieve.

On honesty: these are directional recommendations grounded in the model. Turning them into a business case needs cost inputs this dataset does not contain — imaging lease and read costs, collections staffing, the true value of a retained referral. The analysis says where to look and how much is at stake; it does not claim to have priced the answer.

Modelling

Two facts that deliberately do not touch

Visits and Claims are separate fact tables with no relationship between them. They conform only through shared dimensions — Date, Location, Payer Class.

A visit and a claim are different grains, and a claim can exist without a matching visit in scope. Wiring them together would let someone drop Aging Bucket onto a visit-grain visual and get a number that looks plausible and is wrong.

That decision has a cost, and it is paid explicitly: because claim-grain fields cannot filter visit-grain visuals, the report ships two drillthrough pages rather than one. Claim-side visuals drill to Claim Details, visit-side visuals to Visit Details.

DecisionWhy
Median & P90 via MEDIANX / PERCENTILEX.INC Computed over an eligibility-filtered set, so nulls and non-clinical visits never dilute the statistic.
Deltas anchored to last activity Trailing 30 days against the prior 30, anchored to the last date with real data — a blank tail can't silently flatten a trend.
Unresolved A/R An Open-or-Denied claim calculation, never described as a cash ledger balance.
Native reference labels KPI deltas are real card reference labels driven by DAX colour measures — not text boxes floated on top.
